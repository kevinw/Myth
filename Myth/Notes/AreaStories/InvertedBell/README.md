# The Inverted Bell

This area is a buried ceremonial machine built around a bell that was never meant to make sound. Instead, it reverses the relationship between floor and ceiling. The player first treats inversion as a trick, then as a way to hear the architecture's “other half.”

The route descends three rooms, folds through an inverted parallel, and climbs back beside its entrance. Pale stone, hanging cloth-like vines, and isolated gold lights give way to cold metal near the bell. The pacing alternates contemplation and concentrated spatial puzzles.

## 1. The Unstruck Vestibule

**Mood:** Reverent, empty, and slightly too symmetrical.

**Combination:** NeuralTextureActor, InspectPoint, SavePoint, and two visually mirrored exits.

There is no danger. Neural growth resembles a sound wave frozen across the rear wall, and the architecture establishes repeated shapes that will make later inversions readable.

**Wrinkle:** One mirrored exit is only a shallow alcove containing an upside-down inscription. It is not a secret yet; it plants the expectation that this area must eventually be read from another orientation.

## 2. The Clapper Well

**Mood:** Vertical anticipation.

**Combination:** `burrowInverts`, a looping BurrowLine, and long Ropes.

Burrowing around the well swaps the player's gravity. Ropes remain hanging in world space, so each inversion changes which sections can be reached and where releasing them will send the player.

**Wrinkle:** The obvious complete orbit returns to the starting orientation but below the exit. The useful move is to leave the BurrowLine halfway through, catch a rope while inverted, and allow the next flip to turn a dangerous fall into an upward swing.

## 3. Mirrored Transept

**Mood:** Elegant, mathematical play.

**Combination:** inversion-scoped Bouncers, inversion-scoped MovingPlatforms, and Blobs.

Only one mirrored set of traversal actors exists at a time. Flipping replaces the apparent room without changing its silhouette.

**Wrinkle:** The Blobs belong to the opposite orientation from the safest platforms. A flip that creates footing also removes nearby enemies, but puts their counterparts on the destination ledges. The player chooses whether to fight in one geometry or travel through the other.

## 4. The Iron Choir

**Mood:** Metallic and rhythmic, like machinery practicing music.

**Combination:** MagneticCrawlers, metal slopes, and opposed ConveyorBelts.

The Crawlers treat the angled metal as continuous paths while belts move the player across gaps in that path.

**Wrinkle:** Inversion does not merely move the player; it places the active Crawlers on what has become the player's ceiling. Their predictable movement becomes a moving clock overhead. A belt that resisted progress before the flip becomes the quick route back after it.

## 5. The Bell Throat

**Mood:** Focused danger in a huge dark volume.

**Combination:** Floater laser, falling Droppers, Rope, and inversion.

The Floater's rotating beam defines when the central rope is safe. Droppers punish waiting on the broad platforms.

**Wrinkle:** Flipping gravity does not reset the laser. It changes which platforms provide cover while the beam continues its rotation, effectively shifting the phase of the encounter. The final safe move is to invert while holding the rope, putting the bell itself between player and beam.

## 6. The Aftertone

**Mood:** Near silence, with visual echoes of every prior room.

**Combination:** RevealDoor, Chest, SavePoint, and the opening room's NeuralTextureActor seen from behind.

The route emerges into the false alcove from room 1, now approached upside down. A reveal exposes the neural “sound wave” as roots growing toward the bell rather than away from it.

**Wrinkle:** The chest is reachable immediately, but returning to the save point requires one final voluntary inversion through a harmless loop. The area ends by asking the player to perform its central verb without pressure.

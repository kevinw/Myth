# Common dings, asked as questions

Questions for Human Questions, drawn from negative Steam reviews of metroidvanias, knowledge-driven exploration games, and adjacent deduction games. Started 2026-09-14.

These are prompts for decisions and playtests, not a feature checklist. An intentional tradeoff is a valid answer. Add answers beneath the questions as the game develops; where possible, include what a player actually did and which build they played.

## Why would someone choose this game?

- What will a player remember doing here that they cannot get from another metroidvania?
- Does the opening let them experience that distinction, or ask them to trust that it gets interesting later?
- Does our trailer show the actual balance of exploration, combat, platforming, puzzles, and conversation? Who might buy it expecting a different game?
- Which audience do we actively want, and which complaints are the acceptable cost of serving that audience?

Review prompts: [Haiku's familiarity and lack of surprise](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198262452338/recommended/1231880/); [Islets' friendly presentation versus expected difficulty](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198000031091/recommended/1669420/); [Nine Sols' combat emphasis versus exploration expectations](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197981353800/recommended/1809540/).

## Does our best idea stay interesting?

- Does burrowing create choices in ordinary play, or mostly open specially marked doors?
- What can players discover about an ability after its tutorial? Can it combine with movement, enemies, Bud, or the environment in ways we did not explicitly prescribe?
- Are cooldowns, meters, equipment slots, or swapping steps creating decisions, or making players wait to do the fun thing again?
- Does the safest or most effective strategy use the expressive parts of the game? If players ignore them, what is rewarding that behavior?

Review prompts: [BIOMORPH's repeated form swapping](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198057321625/recommended/1430220/); [Haiku's heat restrictions](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561199203994601/recommended/1231880/); [Vernal Edge's effective strategy displacing its interesting moves](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198158616815/recommended/1546710/).

## Is moving enjoyable before there is a reward?

- Is it fun to cross an empty room with the starting moves? What improves that experience later?
- Can a player explain why a jump, grab, burrow, or attack failed? Are we asking for finer timing than the controls and feedback reliably support?
- Do context-sensitive buttons perform the action the player intended when several actions are possible?
- Can someone play comfortably with each supported input method, including aiming, opening the map, rebinding, and navigating menus?

Review prompts: [Souldiers' ledge input ambiguity](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198042102952/recommended/1419160/); [Lone Fungus' crowded controls](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198129794891/recommended/1674780/); [Rusted Moss controller reports](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561199612476583/recommended/1772830/).

## Is the fifth trip through a room different?

- Once a room is understood, can the player cross it faster, bypass it, or do something newly interesting there?
- Which hazards force waiting on every visit? Does that waiting still serve a purpose after the first successful crossing?
- If we disable fast travel or require a return to Bud or a hub, what changes on that journey to justify it?
- Could someone spend their entire short play session retracing known ground without a worthwhile discovery, decision, or encounter?

Review prompts: [Ghost Song's delivery trips and empty traversal](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198044377336/recommended/347800/); [Aeterna Noctis' repeated platforming](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198965430111/recommended/1517970/); [Animal Well's repeated solved rooms](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198229646720/recommended/813230/).

## Can the player form a useful plan?

- At a blocked route, can they distinguish a missing ability, an unsolved puzzle, and a move they have not mastered?
- After gaining an ability, can they remember or locate somewhere promising to use it without inspecting every dead end again?
- Does the map represent actual reachable exits and useful unfinished business, including within tall or irregular rooms?
- Does opening an ability gate deliver something before presenting another gate? Can the player trust that following a lead was worthwhile?

Review prompts: [Rebel Transmute's repeated dead ends](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197987170102/recommended/1191660/); [The Last Faith's gates behind gates](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561199202669170/recommended/1274600/); [Animal Well's uncertainty about missing tools](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198063872730/recommended/813230/).

## Is the world larger than its ideas?

- What new decision, enemy interaction, spatial idea, or story development earns each stretch of the map its place?
- Could we remove a third of an area's rooms and lose only travel time? Which quiet spaces are actually doing atmospheric work?
- Can players recognize where they are from landmarks and layout, beyond a palette change or the map screen?
- Have we playtested the middle and end as continuous journeys, with ordinary player equipment and fatigue, rather than only testing individual rooms?

Review prompts: [Afterimage's scale and repeated encounters](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197984225014/recommended/1701520/); [The Mobius Machine's exhausting repetition](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197993088234/recommended/2281940/); [Souldiers' long sessions without satisfying progress](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198879852762/recommended/1419160/).

## Does exploration pay off?

- Does a hard-to-reach reward change what the player can do, reveal something they care about, or open a substantial new place?
- Can they feel an upgrade in ordinary play, or does everything still take the same number of hits and attempts?
- Are we making players choose between a moveset they enjoy and the item with adequate numbers? Do upgrade costs discourage experimentation?
- If a secret contains only lore, have we earned enough curiosity for that to be a satisfying reward?

Review prompts: [Afterimage's weapon and talent progression](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198050572726/recommended/1701520/); [Ender Lilies' small rewards versus exploration effort](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561199033601446/recommended/1369630/); [The Mobius Machine's weak-feeling upgrades](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197983374033/recommended/2281940/).

## Are fights asking for thought or repetition?

- After a player understands a regular enemy, how many more identical actions must they perform to kill it?
- Do enemy combinations create readable decisions, or merely stack projectiles, armor, contact damage, and interruptions?
- Does fighting remain worth doing while exploring, or does the game teach players to run past everything?
- Does the final boss test skills the rest of the game has developed? Can players reach it having succeeded with a style it suddenly invalidates?

Review prompts: [Vernal Edge's poise and combat incentives](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198158616815/recommended/1546710/); [The Mobius Machine's repeated shots](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197983374033/recommended/2281940/); [Pronty's final-boss spike](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198041050834/recommended/1286280/); [Nine Sols' late change in demanded skills](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197998040761/recommended/1809540/).

## What does failure cost besides the attempt?

- How long between a mistake and the next chance to practise the thing that caused it, including walking, loading, dialogue, waves, and mastered boss phases?
- Do death penalties make the next attempt harder for the player who was already struggling? What interesting decision does that penalty buy us?
- Can players change approach, explore elsewhere, or use assistance before frustration has already made them stop caring?
- After a difficult win, do they want another challenge, or mainly feel relieved that they never have to do this one again?

Review prompts: [Ghost Song's health-recovery cost](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198044377336/recommended/347800/); [Souldiers' long platforming resets](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561199772193698/recommended/1419160/); [Nine Sols' replaying mastered phases](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198121614744/recommended/1809540/).

## Can the player read the game while playing it?

- Are exits, solid ground, burrowable surfaces, hazards, and decoration distinguishable while moving at full speed?
- Does the camera show the information needed for a landing or dodge before the player must commit?
- Do effects, darkness, low-health overlays, and foreground art preserve the information needed precisely when things become difficult?
- Do animation and sound make successful actions, damage, and interruption unambiguous?

Review prompts: [Laika's camera and obscured landings](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198040881158/recommended/1796220/); [Pronty's visual overlays and interactable objects](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198977905991/recommended/1286280/); [Ori's collision and visual ambiguity](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198019010283/recommended/1057090/).

## Have we earned the player's attention to the story?

- Can the player describe what they want to discover or accomplish without first understanding our invented terminology?
- Does Bud's dialogue react to meaningful events and strengthen the relationship, or interrupt movement to explain things the player already understands?
- Does the world build a question the player wants answered, and do later discoveries actually answer or deepen it?
- Can someone who misses a conversation, skips text, or returns after a week still understand the immediate stakes?

Review prompts: [Afterimage's distant goal and missing smaller objectives](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561199210062007/recommended/1701520/); [Fearmonium's heavy-handed exposition](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198078962041/recommended/1068360/); [8Doors' dialogue and presentation](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197960330148/recommended/668550/).

## Does finishing preserve the good experience?

- Does the main ending provide closure for someone who will never pursue 100%?
- Can an unannounced choice permanently invalidate a quest or ending while the game still encourages the player to pursue it?
- Are optional secrets, completion challenges, and community-scale mysteries clearly different commitments?
- Does cleanup let players apply their accumulated knowledge and powers, or require repeated blind searches and excessive execution for small rewards?

Review prompts: [Blasphemous' silent ending lockouts](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197993370609/recommended/774361/); [Animal Well's completion burden](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198229646720/recommended/813230/); [Lone Fungus' optional challenges and rewards](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198106629973/recommended/1674780/).

## Can players trust their progress to survive?

- Can someone quit immediately after a boss, discovery, or difficult sequence and know what has been saved?
- If the game crashes or a save write is interrupted, can they recover without losing the whole run?
- Can unusual ability orders, missed switches, repeated interactions, or leaving mid-event produce an unrecoverable state?
- Have ordinary players completed the shipped route on the input devices and machines we claim to support?

Review prompts: [Rebel Transmute's reported save loss](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198183905831/recommended/1191660/); [Laika's reported quest blockage](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198027234923/recommended/1796220/); [Blast Brigade's post-boss saving complaint](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198298180525/recommended/1550760/). These are reports of player experiences, not independently reproduced bugs.

## What changes if understanding becomes the upgrade?

This is an additional design axis, not a commitment to remove every item gate. It connects to the [metroidbrainia brainstorm](Attic/METROIDBRAINIA_BRAINSTORM.md). Comparisons below discuss premises and systems without giving puzzle solutions; linked reviews can contain major spoilers.

### What is actually keeping the player out?

| Kind of gate | What must change? | Question for our design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Item or ability | The character acquires a capability. | Does the upgrade enrich ordinary play as well as open a route? |
| Reusable knowledge | The player understands an existing rule or interaction. | Could they apply it somewhere we never explicitly taught them to? |
| Password or fact | The player obtains a particular answer. | Is finding this fact satisfying, and does it teach anything beyond this one lock? |
| Recorded discovery | The game records that a clue or conversation was encountered. | If the player already understands, why must they still visit the designated clue? |
| Execution, timing, or resources | The player can perform or arrange something they already understand. | Is this an intended second challenge, or an obstacle between insight and its payoff? |

- For each important route, which of these gates applies, and can the player tell what kind of obstacle they face?
- If someone knows the solution from the start, will it work? If not, are we really designing a knowledge gate?
- Where would a physical upgrade still be valuable for pacing, visible growth, or introducing possibilities without overwhelming the opening?

### Are we teaching a rule, or concealing an instruction?

- What observable evidence could lead a player to discover the rule without finding our intended explanatory text?
- Can they try it in a simple situation, then make a new prediction that proves their understanding transfers?
- When an early explanation is incomplete, does later evidence refine it coherently, or appear to contradict something we taught as absolute?
- Are crucial clues recognizable through several contexts or routes, so missing one object does not stop the whole investigation?

The distinction is our design interpretation. Toki Tori 2+ is a useful fixed-moveset reference; The Witness reviews show how players can experience learning a more precise rule as the game changing its rules.

### Does a failed experiment produce trustworthy evidence?

- When a player tries the right idea with imperfect timing, position, or aim, can they distinguish execution failure from a wrong theory?
- Can they see enough of the cause and effect to know which assumption to change next?
- If creatures, physics, or NCA behavior vary, which properties remain reliable enough to support deduction? Can we isolate a rule in a repeatable experiment?
- How much setup, travel, waiting, or resource collection stands between a hypothesis and a useful result? What makes the next experiment cheaper after learning something?

Review prompts: [Toki Tori 2+'s fragile puzzle setup](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197971030542/recommended/201420/); [Leap Year's repeated circuits](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198206489113/recommended/2951770/); [Blue Prince's delay between insight and testing](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198038048589/recommended/1569580/).

### Can we support memory without doing the thinking?

- Can the player easily revisit an observation, image, conversation, or location without repeating the journey that first revealed it?
- Does a journal separate observations from the player's hypotheses, rather than silently giving the interpretation away?
- Can Bud offer progressively more specific help only when wanted: recall a lead, point out relevant evidence, then suggest an experiment?
- Can someone return after a week and resume a promising question without needing an external walkthrough?

Review prompts: [Obra Dinn's evidence access](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198871135298/recommended/653530/); [Chants of Sennaar's unwanted confirmations](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198039101570/recommended/1931770/); [Heaven's Vault's interruptions and cross-referencing](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198118840933/recommended/774201/). Optional, graduated help is a proposed response to these tensions, not a feature all reviewers requested.

### What keeps curiosity alive when progress is invisible?

- What concrete mystery can a player care about before understanding the world's history or terminology?
- Does a session leave them with a new fact, a ruled-out theory, or a sharper question even if they opened no door?
- If one lead stalls, are there other meaningful investigations that might supply relevant evidence? What happens near the end when only one remains?
- Are we promising a narrative answer, a mechanical revelation, or the pleasure of solving puzzles? Does the payoff honor that promise?

Review prompts: [Outer Wilds without a compelling personal hook](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198206209553/recommended/753640/); [The Witness's expected narrative payoff](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198033336710/recommended/210970/); [Blue Prince's narrowing late-game options](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198099264520/recommended/1569580/).

### What happens when players learn out of order?

- If someone discovers a rule early, can we reward that insight without requiring the tutorial or clue pickup first?
- If they stumble into a solution by accident or brute force, is there a later situation that lets them recognize and apply the underlying rule?
- Can an early shortcut bypass context or a capability that the rest of the route silently assumes? Can the world and Bud still respond coherently?
- Are essential clues available to players with different hearing, vision, language backgrounds, and input devices, without the alternative presentation simply spelling out the solution?

These are design stress tests, not claims of measured review frequency. The Witness and Obra Dinn accessibility complaints are especially useful reminders that perceiving evidence and reasoning about it are different tasks.

### How could we test this direction in Human Questions?

- Could we prototype the same small route once with an acquired burrow upgrade and once with an always-available interaction learned elsewhere, then compare where fresh players hesitate and what they find satisfying?
- Before intervening, can we ask a stuck tester what they think is possible, what evidence supports that, and what they want to try next?
- After a discovery, can they explain why it worked and apply it in an unfamiliar room? Do they feel newly capable even though no stat or inventory item changed?
- Can we observe genuinely fresh players for each revision, since a tester who already knows the secret cannot reproduce the original learning experience?

## Which knowledge gates could belong specifically to Human Questions?

Working ideas, added 2026-09-14. These are proposals to test, not implemented features or deductions from the Steam review sample. The useful common thread is learning what our existing surfaces, burrow, inversion, and companion can do, then recognizing new opportunities in familiar rooms.

Current grounding: [burrow movement](../Source/Actors/Pawn.Burrow.cs) already follows contours, carries entry momentum, launches relative to the exit surface, and pauses its timer on authored [burrowlines](../Source/Actors/BurrowLine.cs). Lines support branches, room connections, and endpoint switches. Burrow-pop can lift [carryable objects](../Source/Actors/Player.Carry.cs); Bud can ride a burrow and emerge after the player. These affordances are not all necessarily well taught or robust enough for critical-path puzzles yet.

Inversion currently derives a room-local alternate tile field and swaps it with the current one; actors can belong to either state or both. [Room loading](../Source/Game.cs) resets the inversion flag. A continuously traversable, deliberately authored inverted world would extend that model; it is not already a second complete map. See [room inversion](../Source/Assets/Room.cs).

### How could the four starting ideas work?

**Could circling a small solid clockwise N times wind something up?**

The contour-following movement makes this a natural physical verb. A small island could be the axle of an old mechanism: each completed clockwise circuit visibly winds another notch, until its connected gate opens. A spiral or moving demonstration establishes direction; visible notches establish N. Could the same winding rule later raise a bridge, move an aperture, or let the player unwind something counterclockwise?

The risk is an arbitrary movement password: nothing happens until the player guesses the exact ritual. Can the first partial circuit already produce a response? Can mistakes lose a visible amount of winding rather than silently reset everything? Does the required perimeter fit the starting burrow budget, or are we accidentally demanding a capacity pickup too? The winding mechanism and lap detection would be new.

**Could apparently decorative elements point to invisible burrowlines elsewhere?**

This can teach a reusable way of reading the world. For example, a repeated figure looks or points across the room toward a ceiling seam; one early example has a partially exposed line, letting the player connect the image and the route. Later examples can conceal the line completely while preserving the same spatial relationship. Is the rule “follow the pointing direction,” “reflect across the room,” or something else? Pick one clear relation before asking players to generalize it.

Can the player predict a small target region rather than scrub the entire ceiling? Does contact provide immediate feedback even if the entry is slightly off? Burrowlines can already be rendered invisibly, but making an invisible entrance discoverable and forgiving is an authoring and feedback task. Would recognizing the motif later also reveal shortcuts through rooms they thought they had finished?

**Could the player escape an invert room and discover that the entire map has an authored inverted state?**

This is the strongest candidate for the game's central revelation: what seemed like a local room trick becomes a second way to understand the whole world. Could an early invert room show a passage continuing beyond its apparent boundary, with a familiar landmark visible on the other side? The first successful escape should lead somewhere unmistakably authored, with a safe return, so it reads as discovery rather than clipping outside the level.

Can landmarks, sounds, and impossible-looking connections foreshadow the larger geography? Does inversion reveal different routes and meanings, rather than require clearing the same map twice? If this becomes the true path, how do earlier discoveries converge on that inference without one easily missed clue becoming mandatory? Can someone who works it out early continue, and can saving, dying, and crossing rooms preserve a comprehensible state? The full-map proposal deserves a two- or three-room proof before committing to its authoring cost.

**Could understanding the burrow sustain it beyond its apparent limit?**

There is already a useful seed: the timer drains on ordinary surfaces but pauses on authored burrowlines. Could a visible early line demonstrate this through the unchanged meter, teaching players to weave ordinary contours and line segments into routes longer than their nominal capacity permits? Pairing this with the decorative pointing language would let one discovery explain another. That extends the journey by knowledge; it does not refill the meter or add capacity.

If we want active replenishment instead, could a clearly demonstrated interaction restore a little time while remaining burrowed? What prevents it from becoming mandatory button tapping or standing still to refill before every traversal? Does it reward planning or understanding rather than reflexes? Keep capacity pickups and learned extension distinct: can the intended knowledge route work with the starting capacity and generous execution margins?

### What are ten more discoveries we could build around?

1. **Could a burrowline's apparent endpoint actually be a junction?** A route seems to deliver the player to the same exit every time. Holding toward a branch at a recognizable seam redirects them into another passage. Teach it first with a fully visible fork, then let the same junction language expose alternate paths inside familiar walls. Branch steering exists; the discovery should be which connection is available, not an undocumented frame-perfect input. Can the player pause at the junction and choose deliberately?

2. **Could a rock's shape be a launcher rather than an obstacle?** A ceiling or distant alcove is unreachable by jumping, but orbiting a nearby curved solid and emerging from a different face launches the player toward it. The learned rule is that the surface and incoming movement shape the exit, not simply “burrow gives a bigger jump.” Normal-relative popping and momentum already exist, with special handling at corners and upward walls. Can an early safe basin demonstrate the usable launch angles clearly, and can later launch sites offer broad departure windows rather than one magic pixel?

3. **Could quiet burrowing reveal what is connected behind a wall?** Moving makes nearby lamps tremble; stopping near a buried conduit lets a slower pulse travel through connected lamps toward an unseen exit. The player learns to interrogate a surface, then follow the response through several rooms. Nearby torch flicker already exists; connectivity, propagation, and the stopped-state response would be new. Can this distinguish promising walls from inert ones immediately, with visible feedback as well as sound, so it never becomes “wait beside every wall”?

4. **Could a room's empty space be the diagram for its inverted route?** An odd-shaped cavity seems decorative until inversion turns it into the structure the player needs to travel around. Teach the relationship with a small, recognizable shape visible in both states; later, a player can inspect the ordinary room to plan a route they cannot survey easily while inverted. This builds on the existing relationship between solid and empty tiles. Can they predict a destination from geometry, without needing to memorize the whole screen or trust that props invert identically?

5. **Could a burrow bomb be a message sent to the other state?** A buried obstruction is unreachable in its own state. The player plants a bomb at the corresponding position, changes the room before it detonates, and the blast reaches the obstruction. Bombs already have a fuse and affect nearby actors and breakable tiles; deliberately preserving the bomb and defining its effect across inversion would be new. Can a safe example show the countdown and target in both states? Does a failed timing attempt leave the bomb's survival and the spatial correspondence unambiguous?

6. **Could Bud's delayed emergence let one journey do two things?** The player and Bud travel together, but emerge slightly apart in time. At an old two-part mechanism, that separation lets Bud occupy one contact while the player reaches another. Bud's delayed pop is an existing seed, not yet a reliable puzzle contract; contacts, a forgiving hold period, and predictable companion placement would need designing. Could an early harmless machine visibly register the two arrivals, and could later rooms reuse that separation for a lift or a temporary bridge without needing co-op or perfect AI timing?

7. **Could a moving platform briefly complete a burrow route?** A groove on a shuttle lines up with two otherwise disconnected wall channels at opposite ends of its journey. Entering the platform's channel lets the player travel with it and transfer out when the next seam meets. The revelation is that connectivity changes with machinery, rather than every burrowline being a static tunnel. Platforms and line junctions exist separately; a line that moves with its carrier and safely transports its rider would be new. Can the player wait securely aboard, and does the connection visibly meet before the transfer is attempted?

8. **Could something blocking a passage be cargo accessible from underneath?** A crate fills a narrow recess, apparently sealing the way. Burrowing follows the floor beneath it, and popping up lifts it out; carrying it in normally would have prevented using the same moves. The floor walk already ignores carryable objects, and burrow-pop already catches eligible cargo. Can an early half-exposed crate teach this without requiring exact alignment, then let players recognize seemingly sealed storage niches as accessible spaces? Does clearing one reveal a worthwhile route rather than another immediate lock?

9. **Could an enemy demonstrate an entrance we mistake for its private spawn point?** A burrowing creature repeatedly disappears into a patterned patch of terrain and emerges elsewhere. Watching it establishes both endpoints; the player can use the same connection. This would require an explicitly shared traversal rule rather than merely copying an enemy animation. Could the first demonstration occur somewhere safe, and would the marks remain legible after the creature is gone? The reward for observation should be knowing where and how to enter, not waiting for a rare enemy cycle on every revisit.

10. **Could a pond be a road on the way out and a doorway on the way back?** An early route teaches that rolling carries the player across water. Later, a recognizable landmark below that same surface suggests deliberately ending the roll to enter the water and approach a submerged burrow entrance. Rolling on water exists; the underwater connection and its teaching scene would be authored and tested. Can the player safely discover both states in a shallow pool first? This becomes a knowledge gate only if speed and movement state genuinely change which route is available, rather than merely hiding a hole underwater.

### Which experiments would answer the most with the least new machinery?

- Could the pointing motif and the existing line timer pause form one short discovery chain, where the second insight explains how to reach the first secret?
- Could a small winding island test whether a movement ritual feels physically understandable before building a family of such mechanisms?
- Could two connected invert rooms test the larger world's central promise: “this was here all along, and I can go there now”?
- For every candidate above, can a player demonstrate the learned rule in a second, unfamiliar situation, and succeed before finding its intended clue if they independently work it out?

## Research basis and limits

This is qualitative complaint research, not a ranking of games or evidence that a complaint caused poor sales. Reviewers disagree: the Islets sample includes both complaints about excessive difficulty and complaints about insufficient challenge. We should test intended audiences separately rather than average incompatible preferences.

The initial pass retrieved 173 distinct negative reviews across Hollow Knight, Silksong, Ori and the Will of the Wisps, Blasphemous, Nine Sols, Animal Well, Ender Lilies, Axiom Verge, and Bloodstained. It supplied the initial questions about repetition, learning costs, unclear gates, rewards, completion, audience expectations, and readability.

The broader pass retrieved 267 distinct negative reviews across the 19 games below. Review openings were screened across the sample, with selected detailed reviews read in full. It sharpened questions about differentiation, the practical value of signature mechanics, excessive scope, conflicting combat incentives, input ergonomics, and protecting progress. Examples above are illustrative, not frequency estimates.

Collection date: 2026-09-14. Each broader-sample game was queried for up to 10 recent and 10 helpfulness-ranked negative reviews, with the English language filter, all purchase types, Steam's off-topic filter enabled, and a 365-day helpfulness window. Duplicate recommendation IDs were removed. Some queries returned fewer reviews. Jokes, content-free abuse, and non-English text mislabelled as English were not treated as design evidence. The helpfulness window is not an all-time ranking, and recent samples cover different time spans for different games. See [Steam's review endpoint documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/getreviews).

Counts below are all-language, all-time reviews from Steam purchasers, including both recommendations and non-recommendations. They establish a range of public review volume, not unit sales, revenue, budgets, or profitability. The larger titles provide adjacent comparisons; this is not a uniformly unsuccessful cohort. The sample also lacks positive-review controls and a historical comparison, so it cannot establish prevalence or growth of a complaint over time. Old or recently posted reviews may describe older builds.
